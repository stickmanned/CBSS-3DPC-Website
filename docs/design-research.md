# Design research: CBSS 3D Printing Club

**Research date:** 2026-08-13  
**Scope:** Current official websites and first-party Webby Awards pages across nonprofit, education, technology, startup, and community categories. The aim is to extract transferable principles, not reproduce another site's layout.

## Executive recommendation

Give the club a **mature maker-lab identity**: editorial restraint, oversized type, authentic project imagery, and one memorable “layer by layer” interaction. The first viewport should make two actions unmistakable—**Request a print** and **Join the club**—while showing the meeting facts immediately. Interactivity should demonstrate what the club does, not exist as decoration.

The strongest direction is **Precision in Motion**: deep navy/near-black, warm white, one high-energy accent, technical micro-labels, and a hero object that appears to build in printed layers. The site should feel engineered, not corporate; student-made, not childish.

## What “excellent” means

The Webby Awards' 2025/2026 website criteria evaluate content, structure and navigation, visual design, functionality, interactivity, innovation, and overall experience. Their guidance specifically treats clear audience-appropriate content, intuitive navigation, inclusive visual design, fast cross-platform function, and meaningful participation as parts of one experience—not separate polish passes. That is the right framework for this redesign. ([Webby judging criteria](https://www.webbyawards.com/judging-criteria/))

## Transferable design principles

### 1. Establish a hierarchy visitors understand in seconds

Use one identity statement, one explanatory sentence, two action buttons, and a compact meeting strip above the fold. A suitable order is:

1. **Desire:** the surprising object-making hero.
2. **Action:** Request a print / Join the club.
3. **Proof:** real student prints and short maker stories.
4. **Clarity:** the three-step request process and practical expectations.
5. **Community:** meeting information, people, and current activity.
6. **Re-entry:** repeat the two actions at the end.

This adapts the mission-to-action progression on the Obama Foundation homepage, the proof-to-repeat-join pattern on Hack Club, and the proof-plus-Get-Started structure at Harvard Innovation Labs. ([Obama Foundation](https://www.obama.org/), [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com/), [Harvard Innovation Labs](https://innovationlabs.harvard.edu/))

### 2. Make trust visible, specific, and human

Put **Tuesdays, 3:30–4:30 PM · Room 113 Drafting** near the first actions. Identify Mr. Anania as the sponsor and distinguish the student and sponsor contact addresses. Explain what happens after a print request, what files are useful, and that feasibility/timing is confirmed rather than guaranteed.

Use authentic club evidence: a print's name, what problem or idea it began with, who made it (with consent), and a crisp image of the finished object. Named makers and concrete outputs are central to Hack Club's project showcase; Kiva similarly links each action to a person and a specific purpose. ([Hack Club](https://hackclub.com/), [Kiva](https://www.kiva.org/))

### 3. Keep navigation smaller than the club's org chart

Recommended primary navigation: **Request a Print, Gallery, How It Works, Join, About**. Keep Guides and contact details available but secondary. The request action should have persistent button styling, while Join can remain a clear secondary action. NY Robotics demonstrates that a technical community can expose its breadth through four plain-language destinations plus one participation link; X uses an equally compact six-item index despite a large project portfolio. ([NY Robotics](https://nycrobotics.org/), [X](https://x.company/))

### 4. Write calls to action as outcomes

Prefer **Request a Print**, **Join the Club**, **See Student Projects**, and **Learn How Printing Works** over “Learn more” or clever labels. Repeat Request and Join only at natural decision points: hero, after process/proof, and final footer callout. Brooklyn Org, Hack Club, and Harvard Innovation Labs all make participation explicit and resurface it after context has built motivation. ([Brooklyn Org](https://brooklyn.org/), [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com/), [Harvard Innovation Labs](https://innovationlabs.harvard.edu/))

### 5. Use one signature interaction, then exercise restraint

The recommended signature is a **layer-build hero**: a club-made object resolves from contour lines into a finished print as the visitor scrolls or moves a pointer. Subtle layer reveals, button response, and image transitions can echo that idea elsewhere; avoid unrelated particle fields, auto-rotating carousels, and constant parallax.

Motion must remain optional. WCAG 2.2 requires a pause/stop/hide mechanism for non-essential auto-starting movement that lasts more than five seconds, and W3C's motion guidance recommends honoring reduced-motion preferences and removing unnecessary interaction-triggered animation. Brooklyn Org's homepage visibly includes a Pause control. ([WCAG 2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide), [W3C Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), [Brooklyn Org](https://brooklyn.org/))

The 3D/drag experience also needs a static image and non-drag controls; WCAG 2.2 requires functionality that uses dragging to have a single-pointer alternative unless dragging is essential. ([WCAG 2.2 Dragging Movements](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements))

### 6. Treat accessibility as part of the visual system

Target WCAG 2.2 AA: normal text contrast of at least 4.5:1, essential UI component boundaries and states at least 3:1 against adjacent colors, visible keyboard focus that is not hidden by sticky content, and pointer targets at least 24×24 CSS pixels or adequately spaced. For primary buttons, aim for the more comfortable 44×44 CSS-pixel enhanced target. ([WCAG 2.2 Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum), [Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast), [Focus Not Obscured](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum), [Minimum Target Size](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum), [Enhanced Target Size](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-enhanced))

Add a skip link, meaningful headings, keyboard-operable menus and galleries, descriptive alternative text, and a complete reduced-motion treatment. The `prefers-reduced-motion` media feature is widely available and communicates the user's request to minimize non-essential animation. ([MDN prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion))

### 7. Let real objects carry the imagery

Prioritize original photography over generic printer stock: macro shots of layer texture, hands removing supports, a student comparing a model to the print, an object in use, and a wide club-room scene. Caption projects with the object, maker, and intent. The Obama Foundation uses richly described documentary imagery, while Hack Club and Kiva consistently connect images to named people and concrete outcomes. ([Obama Foundation](https://www.obama.org/), [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com/), [Kiva](https://www.kiva.org/))

When original photography is sparse, use a deliberate studio treatment—one background, one light direction, consistent crop—rather than mixing unrelated online images. A rotating 3D model can supplement a real photo, but should not replace evidence that the club and its students exist.

### 8. Make the “wow” fast enough to enjoy

Load the signature 3D experience progressively and keep useful text/actions available before it. Reserve image dimensions to avoid shifts, use responsive image formats, and defer offscreen galleries. As measurable experience gates, Google's current Core Web Vitals guidance recommends LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP of 200 ms or less, and CLS of 0.1 or less at the 75th percentile. ([Google Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals))

## Proposed visual direction: Precision in Motion

- **Palette:** deep navy/graphite and warm white, with one vivid cyan or safety-orange accent. Use the accent primarily for actions, focus, and print-layer details.
- **Typography:** a confident grotesk display face for short headlines; a highly readable neutral sans for body copy; restrained monospaced labels for time, room, file types, and print-stage annotations.
- **Forms:** layer lines, section cuts, build-plate grids, and cropped object silhouettes. Avoid generic blobs and pervasive glassmorphism.
- **Composition:** large quiet fields around one hero object, then a disciplined modular grid for projects and practical information.
- **Motion language:** reveal, stack, slice, and resolve—motions that resemble additive manufacturing. No effect should introduce a second metaphor.
- **Voice:** concise, specific, inviting, and slightly ambitious. Let student work supply the enthusiasm rather than exclamation marks.

Suggested hero copy:

> **Ideas become objects here.**  
> Design, prototype, and bring your next idea to life with the CBSS 3D Printing Club.

Primary actions: **Request a Print** · **Join the Club**  
Utility line: **Tuesdays · 3:30–4:30 PM · Room 113 Drafting**

## Design acceptance checks

- Both primary actions are visible without scrolling on common mobile and desktop viewports.
- The page remains complete and coherent when JavaScript, WebGL, or motion is unavailable.
- Every interaction works by keyboard; dragged/rotated content has an equivalent control.
- Auto-starting motion is short or controllable, and reduced-motion mode removes spatial movement.
- Real club work appears before generic claims or decorative technology imagery.
- Meeting time, room, sponsor, and contacts remain consistent across every page.
- Contrast, focus, target size, responsive layout, and Core Web Vitals are verified before launch.
